HIIT Circuit Card — three athletes, four stations

Small group session, forty-five minutes on the clock including the warm-up and the stretch. There is one rower, one kettlebell, one box and one sled in the corner of the gym, and three of you. That means one station sits empty every round, and it means nobody ever waits on equipment if the rotation is respected.

The rule
Sixty seconds of work, all out, then sixty seconds of rest. Nobody starts a station until the athlete ahead of them has left it. If you are early, stand at the station and breathe; do not start.

Warm-up
Five minutes on the floor together: skipping, hip openers, shoulder pass-throughs, two sets of ten air squats. All three of you start at the same time and finish at the same time.

The stations
Rower: damper on five, pull hard, do not chase the split on the last ten seconds.
Kettlebell swings: one bell, hips not arms, chest tall at the top.
Box step-overs: alternate the lead leg, full extension at the top, no rebound.
Sled push: the length of the turf and back, low hands, short steps.

The rotation
Round 1: A on the rower, B on the kettlebell, C on the box.
Round 2: A on the kettlebell, B on the box, C on the sled.
Round 3: A on the box, B on the sled, C on the rower.
Round 4: A on the sled, B on the rower, C on the kettlebell.

Rest
Sixty seconds of rest on the floor after every station. Walk it off, do not sit down, do not lean on the next piece of equipment while somebody is still using it. There is no rest after the fourth station: that one runs straight into the cooldown.

Cooldown
Ten minutes on the floor when all three of you have finished the fourth station: easy bike or a walk, then quads, hips and thoracic spine. Nobody leaves early; the cooldown starts together.

Coach's note
The sled is the one that wrecks the rotation if somebody drags it. It is the longest reset and it is at the far end of the turf, so the athlete coming off the sled is the one most likely to hold up the person behind them. Watch that hand-off.
