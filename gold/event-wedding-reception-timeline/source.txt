Wedding Reception Run Sheet — Ashgrove Barn, 90 guests

The ceremony breaks at four o'clock and this sheet starts there. One kitchen pass, one bar, one microphone, two servers on the floor, one band. The planner owns the room call; the MC owns everything that goes through the microphone. Nothing on the pass overlaps: the kitchen plates one course at a time and the floor waits for it.

Room
Flip the ceremony room to reception seating: 45 minutes, chairs out, rounds in, linen on.
Dress the tables with centrepieces, place cards and candles; 15 minutes once the flip is done.
Seat the guests when the planner calls the room; 15 minutes to get 90 people down.

Foyer
Open the bar as soon as the ceremony breaks; 20 minutes to set and pour the first round.
Pass canapes in the foyer from twenty minutes in, and keep passing for forty minutes to an hour, however long the photographs take.
Call the room to dinner once the canapes are done; 5 minutes.

Kitchen
Starters plate up fifteen minutes after the room starts seating; 15 minutes on the pass.
Mains go on the pass five minutes after the starter plates are cleared; 30 minutes.
Dessert plates up the moment the cake is cut; 15 minutes.

Floor
Serve the starters; 10 minutes.
Clear the starters twenty minutes after they go out; 10 minutes.
Serve the mains; 15 minutes.
Clear the mains forty minutes after they go out; 10 minutes.
Serve the dessert; 10 minutes.

Programme
Welcome toast as soon as the guests are seated; 5 minutes on the microphone.
First dance once the main plates are cleared; 5 minutes.
Speeches when the MC cues them: two or three of them, fifteen minutes total, twenty-five at the outside.
Cut the cake straight after the speeches; 10 minutes.
Dancing starts ten minutes after the dessert is served and runs until the band's last set.

Notes for the day
The photographs are the only thing we do not control, so the canape window is the slack in the sheet: if the couple are late back from the orchard the bar carries it. Everything downstream of the room call is fixed. If the speeches run long, the kitchen holds the dessert rather than plating it early — the pass is the bottleneck and a plated dessert sitting under a lamp is worse than a five minute gap on the floor.
