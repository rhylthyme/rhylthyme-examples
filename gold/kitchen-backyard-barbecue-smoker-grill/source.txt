Backyard Barbecue: Smoker and Grill

Two cooks, one offset smoker, one kettle grill, one side burner and a cooler for holding. The pork owns the smoker all day; everything else happens in the last two hours.

Pork shoulder
Light the smoker and bring it to 250°F; 45 minutes. While it comes up, trim the shoulder and rub it heavily with salt, pepper and paprika; 25 minutes. Put the shoulder on once the smoker holds temperature and smoke it unwrapped for 6 hours, until the bark is dark and set. Wrap it tightly in butcher paper; 10 minutes. Back on the smoker, wrapped, until the thickest part probes soft — usually another 3 hours, sometimes four. Hold it wrapped in a cooler for one to three hours; it will not get cold and it only gets better. Pull the meat by hand; 30 minutes.

Beans
When the pork goes into the cooler, fry the bacon and onion in the pot on the side burner for 12 minutes, then add the beans, molasses and a ladle of drippings and let them tick over on the lowest flame until you serve.

Slaw
Start the slaw at the same time. Shred the cabbage and carrot; 20 minutes. Whisk the buttermilk dressing; 8 minutes. Dress the slaw and put it in the refrigerator for at least 45 minutes so the cabbage softens.

Grill
Light the kettle an hour before you eat; 25 minutes to a clean bed of coals. Grill the sausages for 14 minutes. Grill the corn for 10 minutes. Toast the buns for 3 minutes, last, so they stay warm.

Table
Set out plates, pickles and sauce once the buns are off; 10 minutes. Call everyone over when both cooks say it is ready; 5 minutes to lay it all out.
